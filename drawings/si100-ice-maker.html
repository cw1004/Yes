Solar adsorption ice maker

Activated carbon and methanol, driven by the sun and nothing else — no pump, no compressor, no electricity. One cycle a day: heat and desorb through the afternoon, cool and adsorb overnight, harvest ice in the morning. It carries 4.9 kg of methanol, which is toxic, flammable and burns with a nearly invisible flame, so every sheet is drawn for an outdoor off-grid machine — all-welded, permanently sealed, with no service port anywhere on the wetted envelope. It is not a domestic appliance and there is no certification path to make it one.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

The whole machine, the Clapeyron cycle and the daily sequence. A wheeled trolley at 91 kg charged — not something a person carries.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

Eight stainless tubes welded to the absorber plate. Stagnation is the design case: 14.0 bar at 150 °C gives a 4.9× margin on tube geometry alone, which is why it is tubes and not a flat box.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

Air-cooled on buoyancy and radiation only — no fan. 1.16 m² of surface for a 174 W mean duty, shaded, with the receiver below it so the loop drains by gravity.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

51 W mean over a twelve-hour night. The evaporator runs at 2.9 kPa absolute, so it is designed as a vacuum vessel rather than a pressure vessel.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

Ten steps to a one-time permanent charge. Charging is the most dangerous operation in the life of the machine, and the charge is specified by mass, never by volume.

Rev A · what this sheet corrects

Hazard data, the marking that goes on the vessel, commissioning, and what to do if the machine is damaged. It states plainly that this is not a certifiable domestic appliance.
